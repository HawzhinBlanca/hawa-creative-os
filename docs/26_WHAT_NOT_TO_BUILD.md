# What Not to Build

## Architecture

- no multi-agent society for routine production;
- no microservice per AI step;
- no Kubernetes for the initial office deployment;
- no generic company-wide “AI brain” that mixes clients;
- no second operational database in Sheets/Drive/chat;
- no unbounded autonomous loops;
- no provider SDK in domain logic;
- no hard-coded permanent model winner.

## Design

- no flat final poster from an image model;
- no generated official logo;
- no factual copy embedded only in pixels;
- no fake “editable PSD/PPTX” claim when layers are flattened or degraded;
- no custom full Canva/Figma clone before testing HyCanvas/fallbacks;
- no forced template route for every creative task;
- no whole-design regeneration for a local correction by default.

## Messaging

- no Slack migration merely for integration convenience;
- no personal WhatsApp account automation;
- no chat platform as task state/source of truth;
- no passive monitoring that silently creates costly work by default;
- no critical approval based solely on an unauthenticated reaction.

## Memory/learning

- no global vector search followed by model filtering;
- no automatic promotion of rejected designs to examples;
- no silent rule rewriting;
- no fine-tuning from every correction;
- no indiscriminate indexing of entire Drives;
- no normalized search text reused as final copy.

## Operations/security

- no auto-updating editor/models/ComfyUI nodes;
- no arbitrary community custom-node installation in production;
- no GPU worker with core credentials;
- no domain-wide Google delegation without proven need;
- no untested backup claim;
- no “exactly once” claim without idempotency and reconciliation;
- no hidden override of failed hard QA;
- no public admin UI when VPN/private access is sufficient.

## Product scope

- no public SaaS billing/multiregion/marketing-site complexity;
- no social publishing in the first pilot;
- no video-production expansion until graphic workflow is stable;
- no feature-count race with Canva/Adobe;
- no attempt to automate taste before capturing strong office examples and reviewer evidence.
