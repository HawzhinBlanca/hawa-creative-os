# ADR-280: Office Photo Library for Text-Only Designs

**Date:** 2026-10-03
**Status:** Implemented on branch `claude/office-photo-library` (from Codex integration head 39452727).
Behind the flag `HAWA_OFFICE_PHOTO_LIBRARY`, default off. Not deployed. No archive is ingested yet:
the office will supply the archive later.
**Owner decision:** 2026-10-02, design item 2. The source of imagery for text-only KAAE designs is the
office's own archive photos. Stock photos and generated scenes were not chosen.
**Requirements:**
- FR-023: a design cites the exact assets it used;
- FR-017: authoritative client assets;
- FR-021: retrieval is scoped to the client before ranking;
- NFR-018: deterministic operations before models.

**Changes a foundation:** no. There is no migration, no new dependency and no model call. Photo
analysis reuses the art-direction analyser (`photo-analysis.ts`) and the renderer's rasteriser.
**Amends:** nothing. It feeds the existing photo recipes (ADR-170, ADR-180, ADR-181) and leaves them unchanged.

## 1. Context

Two findings from 2026-10-02 drive this decision.

- **Blind panel.** Text-only posters plateau at about 5.8/10 overall, and their imagery scores
  3.0/10 (output/research/2026-10-02-design-and-app-ratings/BLIND_PANEL_RESULTS.txt; ADR-271
  section 9). Every judge named "generic template, no visual hook".
- **Office practice.** Every post the office publishes uses a photograph
  (`packages/creative/assets/exemplars/PHOTO_EXEMPLARS.md`). The photo recipes are already the
  system's best output.

The research names the plan item and its rules:

- DESIGN_PIPELINE_AUDIT.md section 4 item 9 is the plan item.
- DESIGN_10_RESEARCH.md section 3 sets the rules:
  - use real KAAE people and events, never AI-generated people;
  - protect only the text zone with a scrim;
  - faces raise engagement.

## 2. Decision

A client can have a photo library: a folder of its archive photos, outside git, with a strict
manifest. When a request carries no picture at all, the flag is on and the client has a library, the
run is given the 1–3 best-matching library photos as content photos. The existing photo recipes
then design with them. The run records the photos' provenance and the reasons each was chosen.

### 2.1 Storage

`<HAWA_OFFICE_PHOTO_LIBRARY_DIR or data/office-photo-library>/<clientId>/` holds:

- `library.json`: the manifest;
- `photos/<sha256>.<jpg|png|webp>`: each photo as stored, upright and renderable (named by the source file's hash);
- `tags-template.csv`: every photo with its current tags, for a person to fill in.

`data/` is gitignored (`.gitignore` line 68). Client media is never committed. Every test photo is
generated by the seeded synthetic fixture.

### 2.2 Manifest schema

The schema is strict zod (`packages/creative/src/studio/office-photo-library.ts`): unknown keys are
refused. The manifest holds `version: 1`, `clientId`, `updatedAt` and up to 5,000 `photos`. Each photo has these fields:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `id` | `olp_` plus the first 16 hex digits of `sha256` |
| `sha256` | Hash of the source file. It is the photo's identity and makes ingestion idempotent |
| `storedSha256` | Hash of the stored file. It is checked every time the photo is loaded |
| `file` | `photos/<sha256>.<ext>`. Only this pattern is accepted, so no path can leave the folder |
| `sourceName` | The file's path inside the ingested folder, for joining a tag sheet |
| `mimeType` | JPEG, PNG or WebP only |
| `fileBytes` | At most 25 MB |
| `width`, `height`, `orientation` | Stored pixel size, at most 16,000 px a side. Orientation must agree with the size |
| `features` (version 1) | Deterministic measurements: `meanLuminance`, `sharpness` (Laplacian variance, 0..1), `soft`, `quietArea` (the calm third for text: top, bottom, left, right or none), `quietLuminance`, `bands`, `salient`, and up to five `dominantColors` with their shares |
| `tags` | Set by a person: `description` (English), `subjects`, `events`, `keywords`, `shot`, `peoplePresent`, `consent` (`granted`, `not_required`, `not_granted` or `unknown`), `usable`, `generated`, `date`, `note` |
| `status`, `excludedReasons` | `usable` exactly when `excludedReasons` is empty |
| `ingestedAt` | When the photo was first ingested |

### 2.3 Eligibility (`officePhotoExclusions`)

These rules are recomputed every time a photo is retrieved; the stored status alone is never trusted.
A photo is usable only when all of the following hold:

- a person marked it `usable: yes`;
- `peoplePresent` is stated;
- if people are shown, `consent` is `granted` or `not_required`;
- `consent` is not `not_granted`;
- it is not marked `generated`;
- its short side is at least 720 px;
- its sharpness is at least 0.3;
- its type is allowed.

An unreviewed photo therefore never reaches a design.

### 2.4 Retrieval (`selectOfficeLibraryPhotos`)

Retrieval is a pure, deterministic function. Its inputs are the copy lines, the brief's subject tags,
its occasion, the request's instructions, the tone words, the language and the canvas size.

The text is turned into terms: Sorani spellings are normalised, English stopwords are dropped and
plurals are made singular. Terms are mapped to event types through a small lexicon: workshop,
conference, field visit, meeting, ceremony, partnership, webinar, accreditation, occasion and call
for applications.

The score is a weighted sum:

| Component | Weight | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | 0.45 | 0.6 × the share of brief terms found in the photo's tags (capped at 4 terms) + 0.4 × a shared event type |
| People | 0.15 | 1 when a people-focused brief meets a photo with people, 0 when it meets one without, 0.5 when the brief is not about people. "People-focused" is read from words such as speaker, delegation, students or award |
| Quality | 0.15 | 0.6 × sharpness (full at 0.45 or more) + 0.4 × resolution (full when no enlargement is needed to cover the canvas, zero at 1.6× or more) |
| Quiet area | 0.10 | 1 when the photo has a calm area for text, else 0.3 |
| Orientation fit | 0.15 | The smaller of the photo's and the canvas's aspect ratios divided by the larger (a 4:5 photo scores 1 on 1080×1350) |

Selection follows these rules:

- A photo with zero relevance is never picked. A photo of a different event is never passed off as
  this one, and with no relevant photo the design stays typographic.
- The best photo is always picked. Up to two more are added if each scores at least 85% of the best.
- Ties are broken by relevance, then by id.

Each pick carries plain-language reasons.

### 2.5 The hook

`apps/core/src/services/design-studio/office-photo-library.ts` is called from two places in
`design-studio-service.ts`:

- `attachOfficeLibraryPhotos`, called right after the ADR-157 photo selection where a run gathers its photos;
- `restoreOfficeLibrarySelection`, called right after the pinned visual inputs are restored.

The hook acts only when all of these hold:

- the flag is on;
- the request is not a website (customer) request;
- the run is past briefing, so the brief records only the requester's own pictures and `photosSent` stays 0;
- the request carries no image of any kind: no photo, reference or logo;
- the client has a library.

When it acts, it does the following:

- **Attaches the photos.** It loads each chosen photo, checks it against its hash, and sets it as a
  content photo. The photo's note says "Office archive photo ..., not sent by the requester", and it
  carries the photo's shot and calm area.
- **Lets the design choose.** It sets `photoSelection` to `{ mode: 'choose', minimum: 1 }`.
- **Records the choice** in `stages.officePhotoLibrary`: provenance `office_library`, the status
  (`attached`, `no_match` or `unavailable`), the manifest's sha256, each photo's id, sha256, score,
  reasons, description and date, and the exclusion counts.

Later stages reload the recorded photos rather than ranking again, so a library edit mid-run cannot
change a run's photos. A photo that changed on disk is recorded as `unavailable`, and nothing is attached.

A revision reuses its parent's library photos. A revision of a design made without them gets none.

The photos enter the run's pinned visual inputs and its brief contract (as asset hashes) like any
content photo. The Desk shows the record (`OfficeLibraryPhotos.tsx`, in the Studio panel).

The requester's note says "The photo on this draft is from the office photo archive, not one you
sent." It leaves out the notes that address the requester's own photos ("send a larger version",
"shown as you sent it").

### 2.6 Safety rules

These are enforced in code and tests:

- copy is never invented or changed (tests check every copy block is set as given);
- library photos never replace or add to requester photos;
- a website request never receives them;
- a photo flagged without consent is never used;
- a photo with people and no recorded consent is never used;
- no generated image is used;
- nothing is uploaded, and ingestion and retrieval call no model.

## 3. Consequences

**Cost.** With the flag on, a text-only KAAE run that receives a library photo takes the
art-directed path. That path makes one layout model call (`art_direction_concepts`) where the
composed text-only poster (ADR-271) makes none. The added cost of that call was not measured here.
It is the same call every photo request already makes. ^[inferred]

**Exemplars.** Exemplar retrieval still uses `brief.photosSent` (0), so a library run is shown the
typographic and office-poster exemplars, not the photo exemplars. Passing the library count there is
a follow-up; it was left alone because Codex is editing that code.

**Language.** Matching on Sorani-only copy relies on the brief's English subject tags and occasion,
or on Sorani tags written by a person. There is no Sorani event lexicon. Adding one needs a native review.

**Faces.** No face boxes are stored in the manifest. Faces are found at layout time by the existing
cut-out service's focus detector, as for requester photos, when that service is configured.

## 5. Owner runbook

1. **Put the archive somewhere on the production machine, outside the repository.** Use original
   camera or phone exports, not designed posts. JPEG, PNG or WebP. Export HEIC photos as JPEG first;
   they are reported as skipped. Subfolders are fine; each file's path is kept as its source name.
2. **Find the client's id.** It is the client's UUID, as shown in the Desk.
3. **Ingest:**

   ```
   pnpm exec tsx scripts/office_photo_library_ingest.ts --client <clientId> --source /path/to/archive
   ```

   This measures and copies every photo into `data/office-photo-library/<clientId>/`, or into
   `HAWA_OFFICE_PHOTO_LIBRARY_DIR` if set (`--library-dir` overrides both). It writes `library.json`
   and `tags-template.csv` and prints a summary. `--dry-run` reports without writing. Running it again
   is safe: photos already in the library are not changed, and new ones are added.
4. **Tag.** Open `tags-template.csv` in a spreadsheet. For each photo you want used, fill in:
   - `usable`: yes;
   - `people`: yes or no;
   - `consent`: granted, not_required (for example, a public ceremony the office was invited to
     photograph) or not_granted;
   - `description`: one English sentence;
   - `subjects`, `events` and `keywords`: separated by `;`, for example `school; k12` and `field visit`;
   - optionally `shot`, one of classroom_or_interior, group_or_crowd, portrait, event_or_stage,
     scenic_or_building, top_down, detail_or_object or other;
   - optionally `date` (YYYY, YYYY-MM or YYYY-MM-DD).

   Mark `usable` no, or consent not_granted, to keep a photo out for good. Leave a row blank to
   leave it unused.
5. **Apply the tags:**

   ```
   pnpm exec tsx scripts/office_photo_library_ingest.ts --client <clientId> --source /path/to/archive --tags /path/to/tags-template.csv
   ```

   The summary shows how many photos are usable. A JSON sheet with the same fields also works
   (`peoplePresent` instead of `people`).
6. **Turn it on.** Set `HAWA_OFFICE_PHOTO_LIBRARY=on` in Core's environment, and
   `HAWA_OFFICE_PHOTO_LIBRARY_DIR=<absolute path>` if the library is not at
   `<Core's working directory>/data/office-photo-library`. Then deploy through the normal release
   process. If Core runs in a container, mount the library folder read-only into it and point
   `HAWA_OFFICE_PHOTO_LIBRARY_DIR` at the mount. This change does not add that deployment wiring; it
   is a step of the deploy that turns the flag on.
7. **Check.** In the Desk Studio panel, a run that used the library shows "Office photo library"
   with the photo ids, descriptions and reasons. To turn it off, unset the flag. Runs already past
   their layout stage keep their pinned photos.

## 6. Model-assisted tagging (plan only; not run; needs owner approval of the spend)

A vision model could propose `description`, `subjects`, `events`, `keywords`, `shot` and
`peoplePresent` for each photo. The proposals would be written to a separate proposals CSV for a
person to accept. A model never sets `usable`, `consent` or `generated`, so no photo becomes usable
without a person.

- **Input per photo:** one downscaled image (about 1024 px on the long side) plus a short
  instruction, about 1,500 input tokens and 250 output tokens. ^[inferred]
- **Cost per photo:**
  - at gpt-6.1-sol list prices ($2 in, $10 out per million tokens), about $0.0055;
  - at gpt-6-astra ($10 in, $50 out), about $0.028.
- **Cost for 2,000 photos:** about $11 on Sol or $55 on Astra. The 5,000-photo maximum is about $28
  on Sol. ^[inferred]
- **Running it would need:**
  - the owner's approval of the spend and the model;
  - a run through the cost governor and resolveModel();
  - a stop at the first refusal, as in the paid-run rules.

  It would also send the office's photos to the provider. That is a privacy decision for the owner,
  separate from the spend.
