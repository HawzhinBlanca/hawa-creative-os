# ADR 155 — Request durability on the one path

Date: 2026-09-30. Status: implemented on branch `claude/fix-durability` from production `6bd479c1`; not deployed.

The 2026-09-30 audit (items 5, 6, 7 and 21, and the durability P2s) found that the request path could lose or strand a request without anyone hearing of it. A Core 401 inside a design (the ADR-146 outage) ended every design `DESIGN_REJECTED` and used one of the office's three retries. The only report of a finished design or delivery was final on any 4xx, so a credential or route fault left the request `designing` for good, with no alert and no Desk action. A Core outage longer than 30 minutes ended `open` and the office's actions for good, so a brief could vanish. A long delivery reason (over Core's 2000 characters) stranded the request in `delivering`. Office alerts reached the first member only, and nobody when that member was the requester. A message Telegram refused for an unknown reason, or a file that could not be read, held its chat's queue for about three hours. Nothing told the office about a request waiting days at a stage only a person moves on. No test ran the worker against Core's real authentication.

Decisions:

1. **A deployment fault is not an outcome.** Core answering 401, 403, or 404 with no problem body (Hono's own answer to an unknown route) says the deployment is wrong, not the request. In the design workflow (`CoreBoundaryError.deploymentFault`) such a step is retried within its own window as an outage is. When the window is spent, the run waits 5 minutes (a durable sleep) and asks again under a new step name (`<step>-after-deployment-fault-<n>`) for as long as the fault lasts. Nothing is reported, and no office retry is used. The first round keeps the old step name, so journals written before this change replay unchanged. The worker's internal Core client (`coreInternalFromEnv`) retries the same three answers instead of making them final. A 404 that Core words as a problem (`Task Not Found`) is still the design's refusal.
2. **The only report stays pending.** `designFinished` and `deliveryFinished` ask Core through `outcomeReportCore`: only a genuine `409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT` is final. Every other refusal, including 401, 403, 5xx and a stale or wrong-stage 409, is asked again until the object's retry policy pauses the invocation after 300 attempts (about five hours). A paused invocation is visible in health and can be resumed once Core is fixed.
3. **No 30-minute limit.** `PROJECT_RETRY` has no `maxRetryDuration`, so a long Core outage pauses `open`, `nativeReview`, `officeDecision`, `officeRetry` and `requesterDecision` rather than failing them.
4. **A refusal for good is never silent.** Where a Core refusal can still end an invocation (opening a brief, a requester's change, the outcome reports), every office member hears which request and task, what Core said, and the requester's own words (up to 800 characters), so nothing they sent is lost. The requester is told the truth in their language with lines the catalogue already had: a brief gets "couldn't start … the office has been told"; a finished design gets "your draft is made, but I couldn't finish saving it" or "I couldn't finish … automatically"; a change gets "I've passed your change to the office". With no office chat configured, the requester of a change is asked to let the office know, and a brief or design says nothing untrue: the failure is logged. An outcome already recorded (a replay with other content) alerts nobody. No new requester line was added, so no Sorani line awaits review.
5. **Deliveries cannot strand.** The outcome's reason is cut to 1900 characters, keeping the leading code Core reads, and at most 50 uncertain names of 500 characters are sent. The claim signature verdict is journaled (`verify-claim`), as OfficeDecisionGateway's is. A replay after a worker-token rotation therefore finishes the delivery it had started.
6. **Every office member hears the alert.** Core's design outcome returns `officeAlerts`, one per member of `TELEGRAM_ALLOWED_USERS`, the requester included when they are a member. It keeps `officeAlert` as the first entry, so a worker from before this change still sends one. `officeAlerted` now says what was sent. The worker sends each alert under `<key>` for the first member and `<key>:<chat>` for the others, so an alert sent before is never sent again. The Delivery workflow's failed alert goes to the signed office chat and then to every member. TelegramSender's uncertain and stuck alerts go to every member except the chat the message was for. An office alert that fails is logged and never alerted in turn.
7. **The list of recipients is journaled.** The office list is read in a journaled step (`office-chats`) in Delivery, TelegramSender and the RequestLifecycle failure path, never beside the journal.
8. **Bounded stuck sends.** An unknown Telegram refusal, or an approved file that cannot be read, is `stuck`. It is tried 8 times, with waits doubling from 5 s up to 5 minutes (about 10 minutes in all). It is then answered `refused`, and the office is alerted. Rate limits, network failures and missing configuration still retry as before.
9. **Stale-request sweep.** Every 15 minutes, beside the Canva sweeper, Core alerts every office member once per request per stage entry (the outbox key names the request and its revision). It covers these waits: `in_review` for 24 h, `approved` and not sent for 4 h, `delivering` for 2 h, `manual` after a failed design for 24 h, and `awaiting_answer` 6 days after the question, one day after the second reminder. Waits older than 14 days are left alone, and at most 5 requests are alerted per pass, so the first pass after a deploy cannot flood the office.
10. **Health counts failed invocations.** `/v1/health` `restateInvocations.failed` counts RequestLifecycle, DesignRun, Delivery, TelegramSender and ChatInbox invocations that Restate still retains as completed with a failure. The count does not change the overall status. OfficeDecisionGateway's refusals of invalid actions are its job and are not counted.

Journal compatibility: the new steps (`verify-claim`, `office-chats`, `office-chats:<step>`, `<step>-after-deployment-fault-<n>`, `send-<n>` for stuck sends) are added on paths the old code never journaled (failures, alerts, and a Delivery's first step). A Delivery in flight across the deploy replays `verify-claim` where it had journaled `prepare`. Blue/green drain keeps such invocations on their original deployment. A Delivery forced across colours would fail its replay and needs a person, as any journal mismatch does.

Not changed: the Telegram poller and intake parsing (other streams). Core's intake office alerts (late changes, requester notes and parked updates in `lifecycle-internal.routes.ts` and `lifecycle-chat-target.ts`) still name only the first member, and none when that member is the requester. Those files belong to the intake streams and are handed off. There is no office group chat setting; "every member" is `TELEGRAM_ALLOWED_USERS`.

Tests: `apps/worker/test/request-durability.test.ts` has 20 tests covering D1 to D6 and D9, including the audit's two proof cases; 18 of the first 19 failed on 6bd479c1 (the other is a guard that a worded 404 stays a refusal), and the requester-change case sends nothing there. `apps/core/test/lifecycle-durability.test.ts` has 6 tests for D5, D7 and the health count; 3 failed on 6bd479c1, and the sweep module did not exist. `apps/worker/test/worker-core-auth-contract.test.ts` (T1) runs the real DesignRun against Core's real `createApp` with no testAuth, in `required` and `trusted_office` (origin `http://127.0.0.1:8080`) modes, with HAWA_BEARER_TOKEN and HAWA_WORKER_TOKEN set. It passes `canva-verify-task-scope` in both modes. With the ADR-146 fix (0905c620) reverted locally, the `trusted_office` case fails with `HTTP 401 AUTHENTICATION_REQUIRED`. Relevant requirements: NFR-001, FR-060, NFR-017.
